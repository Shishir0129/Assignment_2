My analysis focused on stress-testing the structural vulnerabilities of our models and understanding feature interactions under the Naive Bayes conditional independence constraint.
During our experiments, I observed that the model works perfectly when the continuous data matches the Gaussian assumption, but it is highly vulnerable to categorical empty spaces. When evaluating the model without smoothing (α = 0.0), encountering a completely logical but untrained combination—such as "tight sand"—causes an immediate collapse of the mathematical system. Because Naive Bayes relies on joint chain multiplication, multiplying by a single zero-count factor completely wipes out all other evidence. The model outputs a 0.0% sand probability, completely blinding itself to strong, valid physical markers like high porosity heights.
To fix this structural flaw, I implemented Laplace Smoothing (α = 1.0). By introducing a small uniform placeholder fraction (\(\frac{1}{304}\)) to empty categorical levels, we remove the absolute zeros and stabilize the joint calculation. The cost of this fix is a slight estimation bias, as we are technically altering the empirical frequency counts with imaginary data points.
Furthermore, our feature selection tests clearly demonstrate the boundaries of the conditional independence assumption. The physical feature subset ['f1', 'f2'] achieved a stellar Macro F1-Score of ~0.96 because their data configurations match independent Gaussian curves. However, adding geometric channels f3 and f4 sabotaged our validation metrics, dropping performance to ~0.71. In our dataset, f3 and f4 interact via a non-linear geometric XOR rule—meaning they only hold predictive power when read together. Because Naive Bayes is structurally blind to interactive feature pairs and evaluates columns in complete isolation, it reads these channels as pure statistical noise, which corrupts the final multiplication chain.
